## 1. World Description

The Polluted Wetlands is the first biome and the player's introduction to the world. It is a **semi-open, interconnected swamp** divided into several zones that unlock progressively as the player clears Invasive Nests.

**Visual tone:** Dark and murky — brown water, grey sludge, dying reeds, dead floating fish. Scattered patches of color appear where small restoration events have already happened (a hint of green, a flower that survived). As the player clears nests and completes NPC challenges, these color patches slowly expand.

**Environmental Hazards:**
- Toxic sludge pools that drain health slowly if stood in
- Collapsed log bridges that require finding another path around
- Areas of thick fog (low visibility) near Invasive Nests

**Dr. Sterling Radio Callouts:** At key moments, Dr. Sterling's voice comes through the suit radio:
- On spawn: *"Volunteer, your suit is locked to frog form for now. The data's out there. Find it."*
- On first Invasive Nest: *"Those nests are producing the toxins. Clear them out — the land will start healing."*
- On finding first NPC: *"There's a life signature nearby. Not an enemy — approach carefully."*

---

### ⚠️ The Problem — Ecosystem Damage & What Needs Repair

The Polluted Wetlands did not become this way overnight. It is the result of **years of compounding ecological harm** — industrial runoff, unchecked invasive species, and the collapse of native animal populations. The player enters at the tipping point: the wetland is close to a complete die-off.

#### 🧪 Damage to the Ecosystem

| Damage Type | What Happened | Visible Effect in the Level |
|---|---|---|
| **Chemical Pollution** | Industrial waste and agricultural runoff have flooded the wetland with heavy metals, fertilizers, and pesticides. | Brown, opaque water; toxic sludge pools; dead floating fish; no clean water zones at the start |
| **Oxygen Depletion** | Excess nutrients in the water caused algal blooms that, when they died, consumed all dissolved oxygen in the water — a process called **eutrophication**. | Suffocating mudfish; stagnant, foul-smelling water; no visible aquatic life in the deeper zones |
| **Invasive Species Takeover** | Four invasive species — Mosquito Swarms, Giant African Snails, Cane Toads, and Feral Tilapia — have been introduced into the wetland without natural predators to check their growth. They are now reproducing unchecked. | Nests throughout every zone; native animals hiding or gone; enemies actively attacking the remaining survivors |
| **Trophic Cascade Collapse** | With native small fish wiped out by Tilapia, birds like the Purple Heron have lost their food source. The food chain is breaking from the middle. | Tagak hiding in the reeds, unable to hunt; empty skies; no bird activity anywhere in the wetland |
| **Scavenger Displacement** | Water Monitors and other scavengers have been driven away from their territory by the Chimera's toxic spread. Dead organic matter is piling up, triggering dangerous bacterial blooms. | Spreading dark bloom events; bacterial sludge expanding across water surfaces; Bayawak hiding in the deepest reeds |
| **Habitat Destruction** | The reeds and root systems that native frogs and fish depend on for shelter and breeding have been clogged with sludge, suffocated, and crushed. | Dead reeds, collapsed log bridges, reduced plant life |

#### 🐾 Damage to the Species

| Species | Status | What Happened to Them |
|---|---|---|
| **Philippine Tree Frog** *(Lolo Palaka)* | Critically reduced — hiding | Skin absorbs toxins directly from water; population has collapsed as water quality failed |
| **Philippine Mudfish** *(Dalag)* | Endangered locally — suffocating | Dissolved oxygen levels dropped so low that entire juvenile populations are dying without ever being attacked |
| **Purple Heron** *(Tagak)* | Starving — displaced | Native prey fish eliminated by Tilapia; forced out of traditional hunting grounds |
| **Asian Water Monitor** *(Bayawak)* | Isolated — territory lost | Chimera's toxin spread has made its usual range uninhabitable; scavenger role is no longer possible |
| **Native aquatic insects** | Near-total collapse | Replaced by invasive Mosquito Swarms, which carry disease instead of pollinating and feeding the food web |
| **Native fish populations** | Functionally eliminated | Outcompeted and directly predated by Feral Tilapia, which thrive in low-oxygen, polluted water |

#### 🔧 What Needs to Be Repaired

The player's actions throughout the level directly address each repair need below. This is the restoration arc of Level 1:

| Repair Needed | How It Gets Fixed in the Level |
|---|---|
| **Remove the invasive species** | Clearing all Invasive Nests removes the source of the invasive population pressure |
| **Restore dissolved oxygen** | Completing Dalag's Oxygen Defense reduces Tilapia pressure, allowing water oxygen to recover |
| **Rebuild the food chain** | Protecting Tagak and restoring frog and mudfish populations re-links the trophic chain from bottom to top |
| **Restart the scavenger cycle** | Completing Bayawak's Timed Scavenge Race removes the bacterial bloom before it becomes permanent |
| **Neutralize the toxin source** | Defeating the Snail-Toad Chimera eliminates the primary source of ongoing chemical contamination |
| **Restore native habitat** | Each cleared nest triggers a small environmental visual restoration — color returns, plants revive, water clears slightly |

> **🌱 The core promise of Level 1:** By the time the player defeats the Chimera, they have not just won a fight — they have reversed the dominant threats to every surviving native species in the wetland. The final cutscene reflects this: color returns to the water, reeds grow back, and the first clean sky appears above the swamp.

## 2. Map Sections

The map is divided into **4 zones**. Each zone is locked behind Invasive Nest clearance. The player cannot proceed to the next zone until they defeat all nests in the current one.


## 3. Invasive Nests & Grunt Enemies

Invasive Nests are clusters of enemies that the player must defeat using real-time combat. Clearing a nest **restores a small patch of the environment** and **opens the path forward**.

Each nest is visually distinct — a pulsing, dark mass of writhing invasive creatures. Clearing it causes a small visual restoration: brown water turns slightly clear, a dead plant briefly blooms.

### Grunt Enemy Types

| Enemy | Type | Behavior | Ecological Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Invasive Mosquito Swarm** | Swarm | Circles the player, deals small rapid damage, hard to hit individually | Mosquitoes as vectors of disease; the danger of unchecked insect populations |
| **Giant African Snail** | Tank/Slow | Moves slowly toward the player, fires small toxic spit projectiles | Invasive snails as crop and ecosystem destroyers |
| **Cane Toad** | Ranged/Ambush | Hides in sludge, leaps out and poisons the player on contact | Cane toads as one of the world's worst invasive species |
| **Feral Tilapia School** | Fast/Pack | Small fish that rush the player near water edges in coordinated bursts | Invasive tilapia outcompeting native fish for food and space |

### Nest Progression
- **Zone 1:** 2 nests (Mosquito Swarms only — easy introduction)
- **Zone 2:** 3 nests (Mosquito Swarms + Giant African Snails)
- **Zone 3:** 4 nests (all enemy types, mixed together)
- **Zone 4:** No nests — this zone is reserved for the Boss Arena

## 4. The Four NPCs

Each NPC is a **native species survivor** hiding in a secure spot in the biome. They teach a different ecological lesson and give the player a **combat/minigame challenge**. Completing the challenge earns a **Genome Fragment** — a piece of biological data the suit needs to synthesize the Cutaneous Respiration skill.

> **Why 4 NPCs?** The Cutaneous Respiration skill is built from four real biological data points: skin permeability, oxygen diffusion, moisture sensing, and water quality reading. Each NPC contributes one piece of that biological puzzle.

---

### 🐸 NPC 1 — Lolo Palaka (The Elder Tree Frog)
**Location:** Zone 1 — The Stagnant Shore
**Species:** Philippine Tree Frog *(native)*
**Role:** The first NPC the player meets. Acts as the wetland's historian and the player's emotional anchor for the level.

**Lesson:** Frogs as bioindicators
> *"When the water sickens, we sicken first. Our skin tells the truth of the water before any instrument can."*
> Frogs absorb everything through their skin — toxins in the water go directly into their body. A dying frog population means the whole ecosystem is failing.

**Challenge — Biological Control Arena:**
A wave-clearing combat challenge. Mosquito swarms spawn from four corners of a small pond. The player must use the frog's basic attacks and the basic Area-of-Effect (AoE) ability to consume the swarms before they infect the center of the pond (a health-bar mechanic for the pond itself).

- **2 waves** of increasing swarm density
- **Fail condition:** Pond health reaches 0 → restart the wave
- **Teach moment:** Between waves, Lolo Palaka comments: *"You see? One frog can hold back thousands. This is what balance looks like."*

**Reward:** 🧬 **Genome Fragment 1 — Skin Permeability Data**
> *"My people's skin is the most sensitive surface in this swamp. It knows the difference between clean water and death."*

---

### 🐟 NPC 2 — Dalag (The Mudfish Elder)
**Location:** Zone 2 — The Inner Swamp
**Species:** Philippine Mudfish / Dalag *(native)*
**Role:** A gruff, practical survivor. Unlike the Elder Frog who is wise and philosophical, Dalag is blunt and urgent. Teaches about dissolved oxygen and water pollution.

**Lesson:** Dissolved oxygen and how pollution suffocates aquatic life
> *"There's no air left in this water. The foreign fish breathe it all. My young ones are dying not from attack — from nothing to breathe."*
> Polluted water loses dissolved oxygen. Invasive Tilapia consume oxygen aggressively, leaving native fish to suffocate.

**Challenge — Oxygen Defense:**
The player must protect a small school of young native mudfish (shown as small glowing dots in the water) from Feral Tilapia schools. The Tilapia rush in waves, targeting the young mudfish directly. The player uses their frog attacks to intercept the Tilapia before they reach the school.

- **3 waves**, with Tilapia coming from multiple directions in the last wave
- The young mudfish have individual health — if all of them die, the challenge fails
- **Teach moment:** After winning, Dalag says: *"Oxygen is invisible but it is everything. The invaders never conserve it. They just consume."*

**Reward:** 🧬 **Genome Fragment 2 — Oxygen Diffusion Data**
> *"The suit can learn from water too. Every molecule it reads makes it smarter."*

---

### 🦅 NPC 3 — Tagak (The Purple Heron)
**Location:** Zone 3 — The Reed Beds
**Species:** Purple Heron *(native migratory wading bird)*
**Role:** A nervous, cautious bird, hunched in the tall reeds. Teaches about the food chain and what happens when a predator at the top disappears.

**Lesson:** Trophic cascade — what happens when a top predator is removed
> *"I used to hunt here freely. Now the small fish are gone — eaten by the invaders — so I have nothing to hunt. When one link breaks, we all fall."*
> If invasive species wipe out small native fish, the birds that feed on them starve too. The whole chain collapses from one missing piece.

**Challenge — Escort Mission:**
Tagak needs to cross the Reed Beds to reach a safe nesting area. The player must escort him across the zone while fending off ambushes of Cane Toads and Mosquito Swarms that target Tagak specifically. Tagak cannot fight — the player must intercept every attack aimed at him.

- Tagak moves slowly and stops when he is afraid (when enemies are nearby)
- If Tagak's health reaches 0, the challenge fails
- Dynamic: enemies come from unexpected directions, forcing the player to stay alert and mobile
- **Teach moment:** Tagak, once safe: *"I cannot restore the chain alone. But you — you can be everywhere I cannot."*

**Reward:** 🧬 **Genome Fragment 3 — Moisture Sensing Data**
> *"From the heron, the suit learns to read humidity gradients — the same skill frogs use to find safe water."*

---

### 🦎 NPC 4 — Bayawak (The Water Monitor Lizard)
**Location:** Zone 3 — The Reed Beds *(deeper section)*
**Species:** Asian Water Monitor *(Bayawak — native)*
**Role:** A stoic, ancient lizard who has survived by eating whatever remains. Teaches about decomposers, scavengers, and nutrient cycling.

**Lesson:** The role of scavengers in preventing disease and recycling nutrients
> *"I eat the dead. It sounds ugly. But without me, the dead pile up. The bacteria bloom. The water turns black. I am the cleanup crew."*
> Scavengers prevent disease outbreak by removing dead organic matter. Removing them causes toxic bacterial blooms.

**Challenge — Timed Scavenge Race:**
A bacterial bloom is spreading across a section of the swamp, visually shown as a spreading dark cloud moving across the water. The player must dash between marked spots (representing dead organic matter) and "tag" them before the bloom reaches each one. It is a fast, movement-based challenge that rewards the player for being quick and efficient.

- 8 spots to tag, bloom spreading at increasing speed
- Fail: if more than 3 spots are consumed by the bloom, toxic sludge permanently fills the path forward
- **Teach moment:** Bayawak, watching: *"Speed matters. Nature does not wait. The cleanup crew is never late — or the world pays for it."*

**Reward:** 🧬 **Genome Fragment 4 — Water Purity Calibration Data**
> *"The monitor's chemistry tells the suit how to read toxin levels in any water source."*

## 5. Genome Fragment Synthesis

Once all four Genome Fragments are collected, the suit automatically initiates synthesis. Dr. Sterling narrates this over the radio:

> *"Volunteer — I'm reading four complete biological data sets from your suit. Skin permeability, oxygen diffusion, moisture sensing, and water purity calibration. I'm compiling them now. The suit is... learning how to be a frog. Not just look like one — actually function like one."*

A brief visual plays — the suit's display pulses green, data streams across the screen — and the **Cutaneous Respiration** skill unlocks.

### 🧬 Cutaneous Respiration — Full Skill Breakdown

**What it is biologically:**
Frogs absorb water, oxygen, and some toxins directly through their semi-permeable skin. They do not drink — they sit in water and absorb it. This also makes them highly vulnerable to pollution.

**What it does in combat:**
- **Activate:** The player dashes into any water puddle or wet surface on the map
- **Effect:** Rapid health regeneration for 3 seconds while inside the water
- **Secondary effect:** While regenerating, the frog emits a mild AoE toxin pulse around it, dealing small damage to nearby enemies (representing the skin secretions frogs produce as defense)
- **Cooldown:** 8 seconds after leaving the water

**Why it matters for the boss:**
The boss arena has scattered shallow water pools. The player must use these strategically — dash in to heal, pulse toxin, then dash out to attack before the cooldown ends. Fighting the boss without using the water pools is nearly impossible.

## 6. Boss Fight — The Snail-Toad Chimera

### What It Is
A massive, mutated creature composed of the DNA of an invasive Giant African Snail and a Cane Toad. Its body has the heavy, armored shell of the snail fused with the bloated, toxic body of the toad. It moves slowly but hits with devastating force and poisons everything around it.

**Narrative context:** The Chimera has been absorbing the toxic sludge of the wetland for weeks. It is both a product of the pollution and a cause of it — wherever it walks, the ground becomes more poisoned. Defeating it stops the toxin source.

### Boss Arena Layout
The arena is a semi-circular muddy clearing surrounded by dense reeds. The floor has:
- Patches of toxic sludge (damage over time if stood in)
- **Shallow water pools** scattered around — the player's healing zones (Cutaneous Respiration targets)
- Collapsed logs as partial cover for dodging heavy attacks

### Boss Attack Phases

**Phase 1 (Health: 100% → 60%)**
| Attack | Description | How to Counter |
|---|---|---|
| Toxic Spit Volley | Fires 3 arcing sludge balls in a spread pattern | Dodge sideways; predictable wind-up animation |
| Shell Slam | Rears up and slams down in a wide AoE | Roll away from shadow indicator on the ground |
| Sludge Trail | Leaves toxic ground as it walks | Stay to the edges of the arena |

**Phase 2 (Health: 60% → 30%) — Enrage**
The Chimera's shell begins cracking. It moves faster and gains new attacks.

| Attack | Description | How to Counter |
|---|---|---|
| Toxic Nova | Releases a full 360° sludge burst | The only safe spots are the shallow water pools — dive in |
| Rapid Spit Barrage | Fires 6 sludge balls in quick succession | Hide behind collapsed log cover |
| Charge | Rushes across the arena in a straight line | Sidestep; it hits the reed wall and is briefly stunned |

**Phase 3 (Health: 30% → 0%) — Shell Break**
The shell fully cracks. The exposed core underneath is the only damageable hitbox now — the shell deflects all attacks.

| Attack | Description | How to Counter |
|---|---|---|
| Spinning Shell | Rotates rapidly, shell facing outward — touching it damages you | Attack only from the front opening |
| Poison Puddle Summon | Summons 4 new sludge pools around existing water pools | Water pools shrink — the player must be precise |
| Final Slam Sequence | Three consecutive shell slams in a row | Pattern: left, right, center — dodge the sequence |

### Victory Sequence
The Chimera collapses. The suit interface flashes — it is extracting data.

> *"I'm reading it now... yes. There's a corrupted Bird DNA sequence tangled in the Chimera's muscle tissue. Hold still while I purify it."* — Dr. Sterling

A brief cutscene shows the shell cracking open, golden DNA strands rising from it and being absorbed into the suit.

**The suit transforms the player briefly into the Rufous Hornbill** — then immediately back to Frog form — to confirm the sequence is stored.

> *"The Avian Form is locked and ready, Volunteer. But you can't use it here — the suit needs a solid surface to complete the transformation. Get to the edge of the wetland. There's a concrete highway on the other side. That's your runway."*

## 7. Level Flow Summary

A clean top-to-bottom reading of the level from start to finish:

```
SPAWN — Zone 1 (Stagnant Shore)
Dr. Sterling radio intro plays

→ Clear Nest 1 & 2 (Mosquito Swarms)
→ Find Lolo Palaka (Elder Tree Frog)
→ Complete Biological Control Arena challenge
→ Earn Genome Fragment 1 — Skin Permeability Data
→ Zone 2 unlocks

→ Clear Nest 3, 4, 5 (Swarms + Giant Snails)
→ Find Dalag (Mudfish Elder)
→ Complete Oxygen Defense challenge
→ Earn Genome Fragment 2 — Oxygen Diffusion Data
→ Zone 3 unlocks

→ Clear Nest 6, 7, 8, 9 (All enemy types, mixed)
→ Find Tagak (Purple Heron)
→ Complete Escort Mission
→ Earn Genome Fragment 3 — Moisture Sensing Data
→ Find Bayawak (Water Monitor Lizard)
→ Complete Timed Scavenge Race
→ Earn Genome Fragment 4 — Water Purity Calibration Data
→ Zone 4 unlocks

→ SYNTHESIS EVENT
   All 4 Fragments combine → Cutaneous Respiration unlocked
   Dr. Sterling narrates the data compilation

→ Enter The Elder's Hollow (Zone 4)
→ BOSS FIGHT — Snail-Toad Chimera
   Phase 1 → Phase 2 (Enrage) → Phase 3 (Shell Break)
   Use Cutaneous Respiration in water pools to heal and deal AoE damage

→ VICTORY
   Extract corrupted Bird DNA → Avian Form stored in suit
   Dr. Sterling: get to the edge of the wetland

→ CUTSCENE
   The wetland begins healing — color returns, water clears slightly
   Player sees the distant concrete highway at the edge of the map

→ LEVEL 1 COMPLETE
   Proceed to Level 2 — The Fragmented Rainforest
```

> **📝 Designer Note:**
> This level is the tutorial for the entire Ecosystem Hub structure.
> Everything here should feel generous and readable — nests are clearly marked, NPCs are findable without excessive backtracking, and the boss is challenging but forgiving.
> The goal is for the player to leave Level 1 understanding the loop instinctively, so that Levels 2–4 can increase complexity without explanation.

# GENOMORPH — DETAILED QUEST & GAME FLOW

## 1. How I recommend documenting the game

Instead of:

> Quest 1: Investigate the wetlands.

We should write:

> **Quest 1 — First Awakening**
>
> **Estimated duration:** 8–12 minutes
> **Playable form:** Frog
> **Purpose:** Introduce movement, investigation, environmental storytelling, combat, and the player's relationship with Sterling.
> **Primary objective:** Investigate the disturbance.
> **Secondary objective:** Explore the immediate wetland environment.

Then break it down into **beats**.

For example:

| Time      | Player                | World                       | Sterling                     | System            |
| --------- | --------------------- | --------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ----------------- |
| 0:00–0:10 | Gains control         | Frog is in damaged wetland  | Brief radio contact          | Movement unlocked |
| 0:10–0:45 | Moves around          | Pollution visible           | Explains immediate situation | Movement          |
| 0:45–1:30 | Tests movement        | Small environmental details | —                            | Exploration       |
| 1:30–2:00 | Investigates clue     | Environment reacts          | Explains clue                | Investigation     |
| 2:00–2:30 | Moves toward mosquito | Mosquito appears            | Warning                      | Combat            |
| ...       | ...                   | ...                         | ...                          | ...               |

That is the level of detail I think you actually need.

# 2. THE COMPLETE GAME STRUCTURE

The whole game should roughly follow this structure:

```text
PROLOGUE
   ↓
FIRST AWAKENING
   ↓
WETLANDS
   ├── Frog
   ├── Salamander
   └── Water Monitor
          ↓
   WETLAND CHIMERA
          ↓
   WETLAND RESTORATION
          ↓
RAINforest TRANSITION
          ↓
RAINFOREST
   ├── Wild Boar
   ├── Pangolin
   └── Eagle
          ↓
   RAINFOREST CHIMERA
          ↓
   RAINFOREST RESTORATION
          ↓
MOUNTAIN TRANSITION
          ↓
MOUNTAINS
   ├── Tamaraw
   ├── Bear
   └── Goat
          ↓
   MOUNTAIN CHIMERA
          ↓
   MOUNTAIN RESTORATION
          ↓
FURTHER ECOSYSTEMS
          ↓
FINAL GENOMES
          ↓
FINAL ECOLOGICAL CRISIS
          ↓
FINAL CHIMERA / FINAL CONFLICT
          ↓
ENDING
```

The **12 genomes** give us the larger progression.

The important thing is that each biome isn't simply:

> Quest → Quest → Boss → Next biome.

Instead, every biome should have an ecological story.

# 3. THE STRUCTURE OF EVERY BIOME

Each major ecosystem should follow approximately this pattern:

### PHASE A — Arrival

Player enters the ecosystem.

They don't immediately get a giant explanation.

They see:

* environmental conditions
* wildlife
* damaged areas
* signs of human activity
* invasive organisms
* ecosystem behavior

The player asks:

> “What happened here?”

rather than:

> “The game just told me what happened.”

---

### PHASE B — First Species

Player obtains the first species of the biome.

Example:

**Rainforest → Wild Boar**

The player then gets time to actually play as Wild Boar.

No giant tutorial.

---

### PHASE C — Ecosystem Investigation

Player begins discovering:

> Something is wrong here.

Quests become more ecological.

---

### PHASE D — Second Species

The player discovers another genome.

For Rainforest:

**Wild Boar → Pangolin**

Again, the player gets freedom to experiment.

---

### PHASE E — Escalation

The environmental problem becomes worse.

Stronger invasive organisms appear.

Human impact becomes more apparent.

---

### PHASE F — Third Species

The player obtains the final local species.

For Rainforest:

**Pangolin → Eagle**

Now the player has the complete local ecosystem roster.

---

### PHASE G — Major Disturbance

The player realizes something much larger is responsible.

---

### PHASE H — Chimera Territory

The player sees evidence of the Chimera.

But doesn't immediately fight it.

---

### PHASE I — Chimera Battle

Dedicated natural arena.

Three phases.

Player can freely switch between local species + Legacy Genome.

---

### PHASE J — Restoration

Defeat the major threat.

Perform one meaningful restoration action.

Ecosystem begins naturally recovering.

---

### PHASE K — Next Ecosystem

Genome Core points toward the next ecosystem.

Wildlife behavior + geography + biological signal guide the player there.

---

# 16. WHY THIS IS IMPORTANT

We should **not** say:

> “Put your points into Stamina because Frog is an agility class.”

Instead:

> “Choose how you want your Frog to develop.”

For example:

### Player A

Health + Health Recovery

Becomes a more survivable Frog.

### Player B

Attack + Cooldown

More aggressive Frog.

### Player C

Stamina + Stamina Recovery

Highly mobile Frog.

Same species.

Different development.

# 48. QUEST TYPES

To make the full game feel varied, I'd categorize quests into several types.

## 1. Exploration Quests

> Investigate an area.

Player explores.

---

## 2. Investigation Quests

> Determine what is affecting the ecosystem.

Player examines clues.

---

## 3. Wildlife Quests

> Help a native animal.

The player interacts with the ecosystem.

---

## 4. Invasive Species Quests

> Deal with organisms disrupting the habitat.

Combat + ecological observation.

---

## 5. Genome Quests

> Follow a biological signal.

Exploration → discovery → transformation.

---

## 6. Adaptation Quests

Not necessarily formal tutorials.

The environment gives the player a reason to use the new species.

---

## 7. Restoration Quests

Player repairs one critical part of the habitat.

## 8. Chimera Quests
